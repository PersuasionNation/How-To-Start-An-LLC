## How to Start an LLC in 2027, Step by Step

Here is the whole process, start to finish. It is shorter than most guides make it look.

### Step 1: Pick the state where you will form

For most owners, the answer is the state where you live and work. Forming in your home state means one set of fees, one annual report, and no second registration. If you form somewhere else, such as Delaware or Wyoming, and still operate from home, you generally have to register as a "foreign" LLC in your home state as well. That means paying two states.

The filing fee varies a lot, and the yearly cost varies even more. Third-party guides list New Mexico at $50 with no annual report, Wyoming at $100 plus a $60 minimum annual report, and California at $70 plus an $800 minimum yearly franchise tax. Texas lists $300 on its Secretary of State page. Look at the recurring bill before you look at the filing fee.

### Step 2: Choose a name

Your LLC's name must be distinguishable from other entities on file in that state, and it needs a designator such as "LLC" or "Limited Liability Company." Search the Secretary of State's business records before you commit. If you plan to trade under a different brand, you will register that separately as an assumed name, often called a DBA.

### Step 3: Choose your registered agent

Every state requires an LLC to name a registered agent, a person or company with a physical address in the state who accepts lawsuits, subpoenas, and official notices on your behalf. The agent has to be available during normal business hours. A P.O. box does not qualify.

You have two realistic options:

- **Act as your own agent.** It costs nothing, but your name and address go on the public record, and you have to be reachable at that address during business hours.
- **Hire a commercial agent.** You pay a yearly fee, and the agent's address replaces yours on the public record.

If you work from home, travel, or would rather keep your home address off a public database, a commercial agent usually earns its fee. The rest of this guide helps you pick one.

### Step 4: File your formation document

The document is usually called Articles of Organization. Some states call it a Certificate of Formation or Certificate of Organization. It lists the LLC's name, its registered agent, and often its address and management structure. Most states let you file online, which is normally the fastest route. A formation service adds its own fee on top of the state's.

### Step 5: Write an operating agreement

Most states do not require you to file one, and many do not require you to have one. You should have one anyway. It records who owns what percentage, who makes decisions, how profit is split, and what happens when someone leaves. Single-member LLCs benefit too, because it shows you treat the company as a separate entity.

### Step 6: Get your EIN

The IRS issues the Employer Identification Number for free, and you can apply on the [IRS website](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number). Some formation sites charge for it. You never need to. Under the IRS default rules, a single-member LLC is treated as a disregarded entity for income tax and a multi-member LLC is taxed as a partnership.

### Step 7: Open a business bank account

Keep business and personal money apart. Mixing them is the fastest way to weaken the liability protection you formed the LLC to get.

### Step 8: Stay in good standing

This is the step that keeps an LLC alive. Many states require an annual or biennial report. Some charge a franchise or minimum tax. Missing them can mean late fees, loss of good standing, and eventually administrative dissolution. Put every deadline on your own calendar. A provider's reminder email is a courtesy, not a filing.

## Why the Registered Agent Decision Matters More Than It Looks

A registered agent receives service of process. That means lawsuits, subpoenas, and official notices from the state. If the agent is unavailable or the address is stale, the LLC can miss a deadline to respond, and the consequence can be a default judgment against the company.

So the question is not "what is the cheapest agent?" It is "what is the cheapest agent I can trust to keep working?" Three things separate providers.

**The renewal price.** A promotional first-year price that rises at renewal is common. Over three years, ZenBusiness's $99 opener ends up $122 above Northwest's flat $375 total. That is the number to compare, not the first-year price.

**The extra fees.** Several providers charge for things people assume are included: mail scanning past a limit, long legal documents, a mailing address, or a switch away from a prior agent. Ask for these in writing before you pay.

**The exit.** Prepay plans lower the yearly cost but are non-refundable. If you might dissolve the LLC or change agents, the cheaper plan can be the more expensive one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I start an LLC in 2027?

Choose your state, pick a name, name a registered agent, file the Articles of Organization (or your state's equivalent) with the Secretary of State, write an operating agreement, get a free EIN from the IRS, and open a business bank account. Then keep up with your state's annual report and any franchise tax.

### How much does it cost to form an LLC?

It depends on the state. The state filing fee ranges from about $50 in New Mexico to a few hundred dollars elsewhere, and some states add a yearly tax. A formation service charges its own fee on top, and a registered agent plan is a separate recurring cost. Compare the three-year total, not the first-year price.

### Do I need a registered agent to form an LLC?

Yes. Every state requires an LLC to maintain a registered agent with a physical address in the state. You can serve as your own agent if you meet the state's rules, or you can hire a commercial registered agent service.

### Can I be my own registered agent?

In most states, yes, as long as you have a physical address in the state and are available during business hours. The tradeoff is that your name and address become public record, and legal papers will be delivered to you in person.

### What is the difference between a registered agent and a statutory agent?

Nothing meaningful. "Statutory agent," "resident agent," and "agent for service of process" are different names for the same role, depending on the state.

### Do I need an operating agreement?

Many states do not require one, but you should have one. It documents ownership, voting, profit distribution, and what happens if a member leaves. Banks often ask to see it.

### How do I get an EIN for an LLC?

Apply directly with the IRS. It is free and the online application takes a few minutes. Formation services sometimes sell this as an add-on, and you do not need to buy it.

### What is the difference between an LLC and a sole proprietorship?

A sole proprietorship is the default if you do business alone and file nothing. An LLC is a separate legal entity that can protect your personal assets from business debts, with more paperwork and state fees.

### Is an LLC or an S corporation better?

They are not opposites. An LLC is a state-level business structure. An S corporation is a federal tax election that an LLC can make. Whether the election saves you money depends on your profit and what you pay yourself, so talk to a CPA before you choose.

### Should I form an LLC in Delaware or Wyoming instead of my home state?

Usually not, unless you have a specific reason such as outside investors or a holding company structure. If you live and work in another state, you generally have to register there as a foreign LLC too, which means paying two states.

### What is a foreign LLC?

A foreign LLC is an LLC formed in one state that does business in another. "Foreign" here means another state, not another country. The second state typically requires a registration filing, a certificate of good standing from your home state, and a local registered agent.

### Can non-residents or non-U.S. citizens form an LLC?

Yes. There is generally no citizenship requirement to own a U.S. LLC. Owners without a Social Security number or ITIN apply for an EIN a different way, by phone, fax, or mail, rather than the standard online form.

### Can I change registered agents later?

Yes. File a change of registered agent with the Secretary of State, get the new agent's consent, and cancel the old service only after the state confirms the filing. Some providers charge a fee to switch.

### What happens if I miss my annual report?

Many states charge a late fee, and continued non-filing can lead to loss of good standing and administrative dissolution. Reinstating an LLC usually means paying the missed reports plus a reinstatement fee. Check your state's deadline and put it on your calendar.
